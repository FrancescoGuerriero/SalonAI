# AIIBP Tenant AI Training Architecture

## Purpose

AIIBP separates shared AI capability development from vertical/domain adaptation, tenant-specific knowledge and ongoing machine-learning development from business-generated evidence.

The platform uses four governed layers:

1. **General AI** — shared AIIBP capability, tool-use, safety and generic business reasoning.
2. **Vertical AI** — approved domain evidence for Salon, Retail and future verticals.
3. **Tenant AI** — Business/Location-scoped knowledge, policies, catalogues and evaluation evidence.
4. **Continuous ML** — offline candidate model development from governed observations generated by the business.

These layers do not authorise production training, model promotion or production inference activation.

## Data isolation

### General AI

- scope: `platform_global`;
- tenant private data: prohibited;
- intended assets: platform-safe general datasets, evaluation suites, tool-governance tests and generic business knowledge.

### Vertical AI

- scope: `cross_business_deidentified`;
- tenant private data: prohibited unless transformed into an explicitly approved de-identified/shared asset under a recorded governance basis;
- intended assets: domain terminology, benchmark/evaluation data and licensed or approved vertical knowledge.

### Tenant AI

- scope: `single_business`;
- tenant private data: permitted only for the owning Business and authorised Locations;
- intended assets: policies, product/service catalogues, approved FAQs, staff/location context, support content and tenant-specific evaluation suites.

### Continuous ML

- scope: `single_business`;
- intended assets: governed Business/Location-scoped snapshots derived from canonical operational domains;
- training mode: offline candidate development only;
- model replacement: never automatic;
- required lifecycle: dataset provenance -> frozen dataset -> offline training -> baseline/evaluation -> candidate -> human-controlled promotion -> controlled production activation -> monitoring/rollback.

## Tenant profiles

### Stylish — Salon

Training profile: `stylish-ai`

Customer assistant: `ai-receptionist`

Tenant knowledge candidates:

- service catalogue;
- staff and availability;
- products;
- business policies;
- approved FAQs.

Continuous ML task candidates:

- no-show risk;
- demand forecasting;
- retention risk;
- campaign response.

The existing Salon AI Receptionist remains the governed appointment-oriented customer assistant and retains its appointment proposal boundary.

### Gut Instinct — Retail

Training profile: `gut-instinct-ai`

Customer assistant: `retail-customer-concierge`

Tenant knowledge candidates:

- product catalogue;
- delivery and returns policies;
- approved brand content;
- marketplace context;
- approved FAQs.

Continuous ML task candidates:

- retail demand forecasting;
- inventory replenishment;
- retention risk;
- purchase propensity;
- campaign response;
- marketplace performance.

The Retail Customer Concierge is intentionally `draft`. It does not inherit Salon appointment tools. Its future Retail tool family must be separately implemented, tenant/location scoped, permission checked and evaluated before activation.

Planned Retail tools include:

- product catalogue read;
- order status read;
- delivery policy read;
- returns policy read;
- customer-support action proposal.

None of those planned tools are currently activated merely by appearing in the agent contract.

## Dataset contract

`AiTrainingDataset.trainingContext` records:

- training layer;
- training profile key;
- tenant blueprint key;
- production-training authorisation;
- automatic-promotion authorisation;
- automatic-inference-activation authorisation.

All three production authorisation fields default to `false`.

## Production safety boundary

During the current build phase AIIBP must not automatically:

- train against production tenant data;
- combine private data across tenants;
- promote a candidate model;
- replace a production model;
- activate production inference;
- activate a new AI agent;
- permit consequential external actions.

Knowledge/RAG updates, dataset preparation and offline model-development contracts remain distinct from production activation.
